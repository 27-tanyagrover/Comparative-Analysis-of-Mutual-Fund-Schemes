# Mutual Fund Performance – Comparative Analysis

## Project Overview
This project analyses and compares the performance of three mutual fund schemes: Canara Robeco, HDFC, and Parag Parikh, using monthly NAV data from October 2021 to August 2026. The Nifty 500 TRI was used as the market benchmark to evaluate the funds' performance in terms of returns, risk, and risk-adjusted performance.

The objective was to understand which scheme performed best during the study period and whether its returns justified the level of risk taken.

## Analysis Performed
Using Excel, I analysed the following parameters for all three schemes:

- **Annualised Return:** Measured the compounded yearly returns generated by each scheme.
- **Annualised Standard Deviation:** Compared the volatility and consistency of returns.
- **Beta:** Evaluated each scheme's sensitivity to market movements.
- **Sharpe Ratio:** Assessed the excess return generated per unit of total risk.
- **Treynor Ratio:** Evaluated returns relative to systematic market risk.
- **Jensen's Alpha:** Measured performance against the return expected based on market risk.
- **Benchmark Comparison:** Compared each scheme's performance with the Nifty 500 TRI.

The analysis used a 6.5% annual risk-free rate, as specified in the workbook, and compared the results across the same analysis period.

## Key Findings
- **HDFC** emerged as the strongest overall performer, with the highest annualised return of 16.59%, Sharpe ratio of 0.789, Treynor ratio of 0.125, and Jensen's alpha of 6.73%.
- **Parag Parikh** recorded the lowest annualised volatility at 10.65% and the lowest beta at 0.647, making it the most defensive scheme among the three.
- **Canara Robeco** generated an annualised return of 9.06%, below the benchmark return of 10.67%, and recorded a negative Jensen's alpha of -1.23%.
- Both HDFC and Parag Parikh outperformed the benchmark on annualised returns during the selected period.

## Key Learnings
Through this assignment, I learned how to evaluate mutual fund performance beyond absolute returns and understand the importance of risk-adjusted measures in investment analysis.

I developed a practical understanding of how volatility and beta help assess investment risk, how the Sharpe and Treynor ratios compare returns against the risk undertaken, and how Jensen's alpha helps identify performance beyond what market exposure alone would imply.

I also learned how to use Excel-based financial analysis to compare investment alternatives, interpret numerical results, identify performance differences, and draw evidence-based conclusions. One of my main takeaways was that the fund with the highest return is not automatically the best choice; returns must also be evaluated in relation to risk and the investor's objectives.

## Conclusion
Based on the selected period and the measures analysed, HDFC demonstrated the strongest overall risk-adjusted performance, while Parag Parikh was the strongest lower-risk alternative. This project strengthened my ability to interpret financial data, apply investment analysis concepts, and make data-driven comparisons between investment options.

*Note: The findings are specific to the selected period and dataset. Past performance does not guarantee future returns, and the analysis does not independently assess all factors relevant to an investor's suitability.*
